# Electrical Engineering Portfolio

## Selected Projects

### 1. Inverting Op-Amp Design

**Problem:** Develop an inverting operational amplifier and understand the relationship between input and output voltage.

**My Contribution:** Designed the circuit and applied the inverting amplifier gain equation.

**Result:** Developed a working circuit design and analyzed its expected amplification.

**What This Demonstrates:** Understanding of operational amplifier configurations, circuit analysis, and voltage gain.

### 2. Laser Tag PCB

**Problem:** Develop a transmitter and receiver for a functional laser tag gun that receives player frequencies from a great distance and removes noise.

**My Contribution:** Designed first simulation schematic for the receiver, tested physical components using oscilloscope, built the receiver and transmitter schematic on KiCad, and researched and incorporated all footprints, tracks, and measurements for the final PCB design.

**Result:** Developed a working PCB that amplifies receiving player frequencies and eliminated noise through bandpass filter.

**What This Demonstrates:** Experience with oscilloscope testing, simulation, KiCad simulation plus PCB design, and low-pass and high-pass filters.



## Design Journey

At the beginning of this semester, I was still developing my ability to take circuit concepts from equations and turn them into complete engineering designs. I have become more comfortable analyzing circuits, calculating expected behavior, and using simulation to evaluate whether a design meets its intended purpose.

One challenge I encountered was with my breadboard testing. Initially, my simulation was using unrealistic values. This led me to change my whole simulation design before I created another working one. Then when I got to testing my physical replication with the oscilloscope I realized that I needed to revise what I declared as ground and virtual ground. This setback led to much wire rearrangement and clean up, but I eventually found that I had wired something wrong which led to my ground problems. After fixing this wire I again tested my design and found through the oscilloscope that my circuit was amplifying my desired frequencies and eliminating the noise.

Through this process, I learned that designing a circuit requires more than simply calculating component values. It requires testing assumptions, identifying problems, being willing to reevaluate, and making improvements based on actual results and data.

I can now approach circuit-design problems more independently and understand how analysis, simulation, and testing work together in the engineering process.
